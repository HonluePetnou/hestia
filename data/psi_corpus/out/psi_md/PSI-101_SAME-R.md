## Objectives
Similar to the original SAME investigation, the primary objective of the SAME-R investigation was to quantify the sizes that the liquid smoke particulate achieves in low gravity and to relate the droplet size distribution to the smoke generation and smoke transport conditions in the experiment. One of the problems encountered during the SAME investigation was that after the thermal precipitators were returned to the ground, it was found that all the TEM grids were severely contaminated with some unidentified foreign particles, making these grids unusable although some of them showed shapes of certain types of smoke particles. Based on that experience the SAME-R investigation employed several modifications covering new materials, extended operating conditions, and diagnostic equipment.

## Approach
SAME-R utilized the SAME hardware used on-orbit. Based on the experience gained from SAME, SAME-R included the following modifications: 1) a new test material, Pyrell, an intumescent fire-retardant foam by Foamex, was introduced to replace DBP samples, 2) test conditions were broadened including higher heating temperatures and no-flow during sample heating time, and 3) a new instrument, M-pass, developed in-house at GRC, was used to replace one of the two Commercial Diagnostic Enclosures that includes a DustTrak and an ionization detector used in SAME. SAME-R flight tests were conducted from July 16 to September 9, 2010, successfully completing 66 test points. After SAME-R flight tests, three major post-flight ground tests were performed to improve data reduction process and to validate the lognormal distribution assumption, the key to the moment method employed by SAME and SAME-R.

## Hypothesis
Same hypotheses as SAME: in μg, without buoyancy-induced flows, smoke particles from pyrolyzing/smoldering sources grow larger (nucleation, condensation, coagulation) than in 1g, with lower number density for the same mass; this changes smoke detector response and may cause some detectors not to alarm.

## Research Impacts / Earth Benefits
Along with the results from the original SAME investigation, the results from SAME-R can lead to technology for an advanced fire detector for future spacecraft used for long duration missions; quantitative data on detector sensitivity to reduced-gravity smokes; baseline data for future detection technology under the Fire Prevention, Detection, and Suppression (FPDS) program; Earth applications in submarines or underwater laboratories.

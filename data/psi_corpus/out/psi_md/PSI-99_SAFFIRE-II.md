## Objectives
The objectives of SAFFIRE-II are to determine how rapidly a large scale fire grows in low-gravity and to investigate the low-g flammability limits compared to those obtained in NASA's normal gravity material flammability screening test.

## Approach
The Saffire-II experiment was performed on the Orbital Science's Cygnus resupply vehicle after it deberthed from the ISS. As a follow on to the Saffire-I investigation, the Saffire-II investigation added more samples including SIBAL, silicone, structure PMMA, flat PMMA, and PMMA with Nomex. The flight unit had a sample card installed in the middle of a large flow duct. Air is drawn through the flow duct by fans located at the top of the duct with flow straighteners at the bottom of the experiment module. Saffire-II has a sample card that contains 9 samples, each 5 cm x 29 cm. Depending on the sample material, Saffire-II was conducted at an air flow velocity of either 20 or 30 cm/s, the flow being either concurrent or opposed. Once the sample was ignited flame images were captured using two cameras. In addition, thermocouples provided flow, flame, and plume temperatures, two radiometers recorded flame radiation, solid state sensors were used to measure pressure, CO2 and O2 mole fraction, and two anemometers tracked flow velocity.

## Hypothesis
Same as SAFFIRE-I: large differences between fire behavior in normal and reduced gravity mean terrestrial fires and fire standards carry inherent uncertainty for spacecraft fire safety design; large-scale experiments in an expendable spacecraft fill the microgravity data gap without risk to a crew.

## Research Impacts / Earth Benefits
Same as SAFFIRE-I: realistic large-fire study on an exploration vehicle; microgravity flammability limits for several spacecraft materials; validation of NASA's material selection criteria; effect of microgravity and limited oxygen on flame size; Earth benefits for sealed environments (mines, airplanes, submarines).

## Key results (from experimental table)
Silicone samples (0.27, 0.61, 1.03 mm) at 20 cm/s concurrent and ~21.5-22.1% O2: microgravity burn length ~0 (did not sustain), whereas in 1-g the 0.27 mm sample burned completely with acceleratory spread and 0.61 mm burned 7.6 cm at 1.2 mm/s. SIBAL 0.37 mm concurrent at 20 cm/s: burned full 29 cm in microgravity at 2.1 mm/s (145 s burn).

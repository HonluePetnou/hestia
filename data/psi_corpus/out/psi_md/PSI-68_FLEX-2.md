## Objectives
The primary goal of the FLEX-2 flight experiments is to extend the results of FLEX-1 to fuels and environmental conditions that mimic real combustor conditions, as well as provide benchmark experimental data on bi-component droplet combustion over a wide range of initial conditions in order to validate numerical simulations and to develop new theoretical models. Detailed objectives: 1. Investigate the role of liquid-phase mixing on the burning characteristics of bi-component fuel droplets. 2. Examine the steady and unsteady liquid and gas-phase phenomena in pure fuel combustion. 3. Study flame extinction mechanisms and their boundaries. 4. Analyze the soot formation process, including soot volume fraction and soot-shell dynamics. 5. Determine the influence of droplet interactions on the flammability limits and extinction behavior of droplet arrays. 6. Validate theoretical models of droplet combustion through comprehensive experimental data collection in microgravity environments.

## Approach
The chamber was filled with the appropriate atmosphere, varied in pressure between 0.5 atm and 3.0 atm and O2 concentration between 0.1 and 0.4 mole fraction with the diluent gas making up the balance. A settling time of approximately 2 minutes ensured stable temperature and pressure. A predetermined amount of fuel (based on target droplet size) was dispensed onto the support fiber for tests where the droplet is not freely deployed; after needle retraction, a dwell of at least 10 seconds let internal fluid motion subside. The ignitor was powered for 1 to 5 seconds then retracted. If the flow field was generated by translating the droplet, motion began when the ignitor retracted. Near real-time color video verified deployment, ignition and progress; chamber pressure and temperature were recorded. Steps: determine test parameters (pressure, temperature, fuel mixture, humidity); fill chamber; deploy droplet(s) controlling diameter; stabilize; ignite; track flame evolution with high-speed photography; adjust; analyze data.

## Hypothesis
Sophisticated numerical simulations of convective droplet combustion now include full Navier-Stokes gas-phase flow, but use approximations for chemistry, radiation, and gas-liquid coupling whose validity has not been confirmed with benchmark data. In microgravity, buoyancy is minimized, and the droplet can be characterized only as a function of thermocapillary or solutal-capillary stresses, allowing clear observation of fundamental processes, including transitions between radiative and diffusive extinction of the flame. The goal is benchmark data on bi-component droplet combustion to validate simulations and develop new theoretical models.

## Research Impacts / Earth Benefits
Liquid fuel combustion is the overwhelming energy source in transportation. Benefits: more sustainable energy production; combustor designs with minimal carbon footprint, maximum fuel efficiency and fewer pollutant emissions; quantitative evaluation of future fuels through surrogates (mixtures of pure fuels that simulate real fuels); validation of jet engine models by engine manufacturers.

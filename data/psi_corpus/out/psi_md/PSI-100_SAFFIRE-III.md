## Objectives
The objectives of this investigation were to: (1) determine how rapidly a large scale fire grows in low-gravity and (2) investigate the low-g flammability limits compared to those obtained in NASA's normal gravity material flammability screening test.

## Approach
The Saffire-III experiments were performed on the Orbital Science's Cygnus resupply vehicle after it deberthed from the ISS. As a follow on to the Saffire 1 investigation, the Saffire III investigation also used SIBAL as the sample material. However, the experiments were conducted at a flow velocity of 30 cm/s compared to 20 cm/s during Saffire I. The flow was either concurrent or opposed. Once the sample was ignited flame images were captured using two cameras. In addition, thermocouples provided flow, flame, and plume temperatures, two radiometers recorded flame radiation, solid state sensors were used to measure pressure, CO2 and O2 mole fraction, and two anemometers tracked flow velocity.

## Hypothesis
Same as SAFFIRE-I (terrestrial fire standards carry inherent uncertainty for spacecraft; large-scale tests in an expendable spacecraft).

## Research Impacts / Earth Benefits
Same as SAFFIRE-I (realistic large-fire data, microgravity flammability limits, validation of material selection criteria, Earth benefits for sealed environments).

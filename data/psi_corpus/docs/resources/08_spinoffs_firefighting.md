# NASA Spinoffs related to fire safety on Earth

## Fireman's Air Tanks (originally published 1976) — https://spinoff.nasa.gov/node/9582
Together with NASA's Johnson Space Center, A-T-O Inc.'s Scott Aviation developed light-weight firefighter's air tanks. The backpack system weighs only 20 pounds for a 30-minute air supply, 13 pounds less than conventional firefighting tanks. They are pressurized at 4,500 psi (twice current tanks), made of an aluminum liner wrapped by resin-impregnated glass fibers, eliminating corrosion and lightening the load. A redesigned face mask permits better vision. The low-air warning device is personalized so it can't be heard by others, reducing confusion. Structural Composites Inc., The Boeing Co., and Martin-Marietta Corp. developed uses for this technology. Full article: http://hdl.handle.net/hdl:2060/20030005480

## Fire Detector (originally published 1978) — https://spinoff.nasa.gov/node/9407
The Brunswick Incipient Fire Detector, originally used in space shuttle orbiters, samples air to determine the presence of combustion particles and, unlike earlier equipment, can discriminate between combustion particles and extraneous matter such as dust, making it less susceptible to false alarms. For large areas such as mines, any number of sensors can be linked to a central processor/display to provide a central fire watch system that instantly warns of incipient fire and pinpoints its location. It senses and signals a fire condition before the appearance of flame and smoke. Full article: http://hdl.handle.net/hdl:2060/20070018916

## New Technology for the Fire Attire (originally published 1982) — https://spinoff.nasa.gov/node/9918
Project FIRES (Firefighter's Integrated Response Equipment System), jointly sponsored by NASA and FEMA's U.S. Fire Administration with technical management by Marshall Space Flight Center, applies advanced materials and design concepts derived from Apollo to update firefighter gear that did not adequately protect against many fire-suppression hazards. A major focus is lightweight, fire-resistant, heat-protective materials originally developed for astronauts' space suits or spacecraft components requiring thermal protection. Data from fourteen municipal fire departments in the field evaluation form a basis for new nationwide protective ensemble standards. Full article: http://hdl.handle.net/hdl:2060/20030002834
